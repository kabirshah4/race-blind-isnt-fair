Which lenders deserve a closer look?

For each lender, we compare how often it denied Black and white applicants with how often their finances alone would predict. A gap that finances don’t explain is a reason to look closer, not proof of discrimination.

1 · Raw gap

How many times more often the lender denied Black applicants than white ones. Part of this can be explained by income, debts and loan size.

2 · After finances

A model that sees only finances (no race, no neighborhood) predicts each applicant’s chance of denial. We divide each group’s real denials by its predicted ones. 1.0× means finances explain the gap.

3 · Look closer

The bar is the 95% range. Look closer means the whole range sits above 1.0×, even after correcting for testing dozens of lenders at once.

What this can and can’t tell you

Public mortgage data has no credit scores. A lender whose Black applicants have weaker credit would show a gap here even if it treats everyone the same. So a flag means “pull the files and check,” which is what a fair-lending analyst would do next.

Lenders are anonymised and numbered by size (Lender 1 = most applications). We only include lenders with at least 50 Black and 50 white applications and 5 denials in each group.

An analyst can run the same check on any one lender with python src/audit_lender.py --lei <lender ID>. Method and code: github.com/kabirshah4/race-blind-isnt-fair.

Data: public HMDA records, CFPB / FFIEC (ffiec.cfpb.gov), 2022 to train the finances-only model, 2023 to audit. Team KV Gods · Veritas AI High School Hackathon 2026 · Prototype, not legal or financial advice.
